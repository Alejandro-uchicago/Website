About

I develop deep learning and computer vision methods for medical image analysis, with a focus on the geometry of anatomical structures.

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow in the Department of Neurological Surgery at the University of Chicago, working on data-driven models of pediatric cranial development and surgical outcomes in craniosynostosis. Previously I was a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Biostatistics and Informatics at the University of Colorado Anschutz Medical Campus.

I earned my PhD in Engineering Sciences (Biomedical Engineering emphasis, with honors) from the Autonomous University of San Luis Potosí, where my dissertation developed blind decomposition methods for end-member and abundance estimation in medical imaging. My work spans geometric deep learning on meshes, hyperspectral imaging, blind spectral unmixing, and generative models for clinical applications.

Research

Geometric learning on 3D surfaces

Graph autoencoders for surface encoding and mesh representation transfer, and mesh registration via geometric feature homogenization — applied to 3D photogrammetry of the head.

Pediatric cranial modeling

Data-driven prediction of personalized head development and surgical outcomes in craniosynostosis, from cross-sectional pediatric imaging.

Hyperspectral imaging & unmixing

Blind linear and nonlinear spectral unmixing with spatial coherence for intraoperative brain-tumor detection and histopathology classification.

Generative & predictive models

Transformer and generative architectures with adversarial training to synthesize and forecast pathological anatomical development.
